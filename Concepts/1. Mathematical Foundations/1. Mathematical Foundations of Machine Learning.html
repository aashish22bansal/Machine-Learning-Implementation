Mathematical Foundations of Machine Learning

A structured, first-principles overview of the mathematics that power modern machine learning algorithms.

1. Linear Algebra — The Language of ML

  • Vectors and vector spaces
  • Matrices and matrix multiplication
  • Dot product and projections
  • Eigenvalues and eigenvectors
  • Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)
  • Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
ML models mostly learn optimal linear transformations of data.

2. Probability Theory — Modeling Uncertainty

  • Random variables (discrete & continuous)
  • Probability distributions (Bernoulli, Gaussian, Poisson)
  • Expectation and variance
  • Conditional probability
  • Bayes’ Theorem
Learning is estimating parameters that maximize probability.

3. Statistics — Learning from Data

  • Descriptive vs inferential statistics
  • Sampling and estimators
  • Bias–variance tradeoff
  • Hypothesis testing
  • Confidence intervals
  • Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE)
Good ML is statistically valid ML.

4. Calculus — How Models Learn

  • Derivatives and gradients
  • Partial derivatives
  • Chain rule
  • Jacobian and Hessian matrices
  • Convex vs non-convex functions
Training a model is controlled gradient-based optimization.

5. Optimization Theory — Finding the Best Model

  • Convex optimization
  • Local vs global minima
  • Gradient Descent, SGD, Adam
  • Lagrange multipliers
  • Regularization (L1, L2)
Better optimization yields faster, more stable learning.

6. Information Theory — Measuring Learning Quality

  • Entropy
  • Cross-entropy loss
  • Kullback–Leibler divergence
  • Mutual information
Learning reduces uncertainty in predictions.

7. Geometry & High-Dimensional Intuition

  • Distance metrics
  • Cosine similarity
  • Manifold hypothesis
  • Embedding spaces
High-dimensional data often lies on lower-dimensional manifolds.
